# M6 — energy + robustness of the navigation-layer controllers (H2, H4)

_2026-09-17 · headline: **H2 holds convincingly on the navigation layer** (frozen SNN
10.4× cheaper than the MLP on the literature-standard metric, 2.4× on full honest
accounting) — the clean contrast with [L5](../L5_energy/README.md)'s marginal 1.04× on
locomotion. But two honest findings cut against the easy story: **per-decision efficiency
does not survive conversion to per-task efficiency**, and **online plasticity is not
free** — R-STDP's dense update costs 12× the MLP's entire forward pass, which is exactly
what the FPGA (M7/M8) has to fix._

Generated numbers live in [`results.md`](results.md) (regenerated by
`scripts/m6_energy.py`); this file is the analysis. Method and per-op costs:
[`src/nmc/eval/energy.py`](../../src/nmc/eval/energy.py).

## Method, and how it differs from the literature's

Same base as L5 so the two tracks are comparable — SynOps × E_AC for the SNN, MACs ×
E_MAC for the ANN, 45 nm Horowitz (2014) per-op costs, firing rates **measured on real
closed-loop rollouts** (3 seeds × 10 eps, dropout = 0.20). Three deliberate additions,
because the plain model flatters SNNs and this thesis should not:

1. **Neuron-state updates**, paid every tick for every neuron whether it spikes or not.
2. **The spike encoder**, which the MLP never pays.
3. **The cost of online learning itself** — R-STDP, TD(λ) backprop, TM-NORM
   recalibration — with R-STDP costed *twice*: as the dense reference implementation
   actually runs, and as the **event-driven lower bound** a crossbar/FPGA would reach.

## Result 1 — H2 holds on navigation (unlike locomotion)

| view | frozen SNN | frozen MLP | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| literature-standard (SynOps vs MACs only) | 125.5 nJ | 1304.8 nJ | **10.4× cheaper** |
| full accounting (+ neurons, + encoder) | 549.7 nJ | 1321.7 nJ | **2.4× cheaper** |

L5 found the *locomotion* spiking policy only 1.04× cheaper, and only after sparsity
regularization and minimising T. Navigation gets an order of magnitude on the same
metric. This is the sharpest quantitative form yet of the thesis's layer-specific
message: **sparse spike coding suits coarse, discrete navigation and does not suit
continuous fine motor control** (cf. [D13/D15](../../docs/references/sota_decisions.md)).

## Result 2 — at 1.5% firing, the neurons cost more than the synapses

Frozen SNN's 549.7 nJ splits as **126 nJ synapses / 420 nJ neuron-state updates / 4 nJ
encoder**. The per-tick membrane and adaptive-threshold arithmetic — paid by all 1088
neurons across all T=20 ticks regardless of activity — is **3.4× the entire synaptic
cost**. Reporting SynOps alone, as the literature usually does, would have hidden this
completely.

It is also actionable: at this sparsity the lever that matters is not further reducing
firing but reducing T, or making neuron updates event-driven. (L5 found the same about
T from the opposite direction: T was the dominant energy knob for locomotion too.)

## Result 3 — online plasticity is expensive, and that is the FPGA's job

| controller | inference | learning | total | vs frozen MLP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen SNN | 126 nJ | — | 550 nJ | 2.40× cheaper |
| R-STDP (dense, as implemented) | 125 nJ | **15 538 nJ** | 16 088 nJ | 0.08× (**12× COSTLIER**) |
| ↳ R-STDP (event-driven bound) | 125 nJ | **747 nJ** | 1 297 nJ | 1.02× cheaper |
| Online MLP (TD(λ) backprop) | 1322 nJ | **7 587 nJ** | 8 908 nJ | 0.15× (6.7× costlier) |

Three claims fall out, and they are the most FPGA-relevant numbers the project has
produced so far:

- The **dense eligibility update dominates everything** — 28× the network's own
  inference cost. Any "SNNs are efficient" claim that ignores the learning rule is
  wrong by an order of magnitude for an *online-learning* controller.
- The **event-driven formulation is a 20.8× reduction** (15 538 → 747 nJ), which brings
  an *online-learning* R-STDP controller to **parity with a frozen MLP forward pass**.
  That is the quantified target for M7/M8, replacing the previous hand-wave.
- **Event-driven R-STDP learning is 10.2× cheaper than backprop learning** (747 nJ vs
  7 587 nJ). This is the concrete version of the neuromorphic online-learning pitch:
  local three-factor updates vs global gradients.

## Result 4 (the honest one) — per-decision efficiency ≠ per-task efficiency

| controller | nJ/decision | decisions/ep | success (M5 @0.20) | **µJ per success** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen MLP | 1321.7 | 586 | 34.0% | **2280** |
| Frozen SNN | 549.7 | 882 | 21.3% | **2276** |

The frozen SNN's 2.4× per-decision advantage is **exactly cancelled** by its lower
success rate and longer paths: 2276 vs 2280 µJ per successful navigation — a dead heat.
Efficiency claims quoted per inference are not deployment claims, and the nav layer is a
clean worked example. (R-STDP, at 64 200 µJ/success under the dense update, is two orders
off; TM-NORM's 1.7% success makes its cheap inference irrelevant.)

## Result 5 — M4b-6: no firing-rate renormalization

| distribution | mean rate | Wasserstein vs clean |
|---|---|---|
| frozen SNN, clean | 1.52% | 0 (reference) |
| frozen SNN, shifted | 1.51% | 2.45e-04 |
| R-STDP, shifted (late eps) | 1.49% | 3.06e-04 |

R-STDP does **not** move spike statistics back toward the clean reference — it ends up
marginally further away. Equally important: **the shift barely perturbs the frozen
network's firing statistics in the first place** (1.52% → 1.51%). That independently
explains why TM-NORM-style threshold renormalization was never going to rescue this
shift, and it is consistent with M5's finding that R-STDP's adaptation does not help
here — there is no statistical distortion for it to undo.

## Result 6 — H4 REFUTED: the MLP degrades *more* gracefully than the SNN

Additive LiDAR noise swept on the base distribution (no dropout shift) so that
noise-robustness is isolated from shift-recovery. 5 seeds × 10 eps per point, 95% CI
across seeds. Stitched from two runs with identical seeds/config:
`noise_sweep.md` (σ ≤ 0.2) and `noise_sweep_high.md` (σ ≥ 0.3).

| controller | σ=0 | σ=0.05 | σ=0.1 | σ=0.2 | σ=0.3 | σ=0.5 | σ=0.8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Frozen MLP** | 42% | 50% | 52% | 34% | 42% | 34% | **38%** |
| Online MLP | 48% | 48% | 42% | 36% | 40% | 36% | 10% |
| **Frozen SNN** | 40% | 42% | 50% | 40% | 28% | 18% | **18%** |
| Pure-STDP SNN | 14% | 16% | 8% | 4% | 16% | 8% | 6% |
| R-STDP SNN | 42% | 54% | 48% | 30% | 28% | 36% | 34% |
| TM-NORM SNN | 2% | 20% | 20% | 14% | 14% | 4% | 0% |

Up to σ ≤ 0.2 nothing separates (the earlier "inconclusive" reading was correct: the
range was too mild). Once the perturbation actually bites, the ordering is the **opposite
of the H4 hypothesis**: the **frozen MLP is essentially noise-immune across the whole
range** (42% → 38% at σ=0.8, slope −0.6 pts/0.1σ) while the **frozen SNN falls by more
than half** (≈45% → 18%, slope −1.8). Online MLP collapses only at the extreme
(N50 = 0.685), and TM-NORM dies completely (0% at σ=0.8).

**Interpretation — this is a rate-coding cost, and it is mechanistically coherent.** The
SNN does not read the LiDAR value; it *samples* it, Poisson-rate-coding nearness over
T=20 ticks. The rate estimate already carries sampling noise, so additive input noise
compounds with encoding noise instead of being averaged away. The MLP consumes the analog
value directly and never pays that. The usual neuromorphic claim (temporal filtering and
population coding average noise out) does not hold here at T=20 — and the fix, if the
thesis wants one, is the same knob L5 and Result 2 above both point at: T (more ticks =
better rate estimate = more energy), or a non-stochastic encoder ([D4](../../docs/references/sota_decisions.md)
already flags latency/learned encoding as the SOTA alternative to revisit).

So the nav layer's neuromorphic scorecard is **mixed and specific**: energy — real win
(10.4×); shift-robustness from ALIF neuron dynamics — real win ([M4b-7](../M4b_extras/README.md),
+10 pts); noise robustness — **loss**; online-plasticity recovery — no effect
([M5](../M5_full_comparison/README.md)).
